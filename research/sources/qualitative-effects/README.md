# Two human questions about one situation

Lin, Tafjord, Clark and Gardner asked writers to describe a situation that illustrates a relation from a background passage and ask questions about its effects. We retain the unaltered background, situation, question IDs and all published answers from the original ROPES train and development archive. The publisher identifies the data license as CC BY 4.0. [Original paper](https://aclanthology.org/D19-5808/) · [Publisher dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/ropes)

The original training and development files share 42 normalized background passages. Our 24 held and 192 training backgrounds are distinct. For each situation we choose an adjacent pair of original questions with different annotated answers and at least 0.35 Jaccard question-word similarity. We take the closest qualifying pair, resolving ties by hash. An independent hash determines the source order. We group and deduplicate by background, situation and question across cohorts. We exclude near-identical source paragraphs across the split. At most 3.95% of the shorter paragraph’s distinct five-word sequences occur in the other paragraph on the selected opposite-split comparisons.

The paired questions test sensitivity to the actual question. Some pairs change one named alternative, while others reverse a relation. Both answers must match their own publisher annotations to count as a whole pair. Run `.venv/bin/python -m scripts.qualitative_sources --acquire` to rebuild the enrollment from the hash-pinned original. This enrollment uses no model predictions.
