# Source-backed answers that require more than a single cell

Nan and colleagues collected free-form questions, human-written answers and highlighted supporting cells for Wikipedia tables. A fixed selection of 384 training and 24 test questions preserves the full tables, original page and section labels, and original human wording. Each source reference contains at least two distinct highlighted values absent from the user question. The 24 test questions come from 24 different pages. No normalized page title, complete normalized table or normalized question repeats between the cohorts or overlaps the earlier 280 changed-table questions. [FeTaQA paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.3/) · [Pinned data](https://github.com/Yale-LILY/FeTaQA/tree/bbc441be807212d736c8c35a18146b530dde11d0) · [CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE)

Eligibility uses complete rectangular, mountable tables of at most 40 rows and 12 columns. The English-language normalization preserves signed numbers, decimal points, percentages and currency symbols. Training and test candidates retain their respective publisher splits. Hash order decides among eligible records. Exact cell matching can miss paraphrases or credit an incorrect statement that happens to mention an entity. All 24 held questions therefore have a source-only assessment identifying the actual facts the user asks for. The original records and annotations stay available for comparison. [Counts and hashes](qualification.json) · [Source material](originals.json) · [Full source review](source-review.json)

For example, one question asks which role Christian Antidormi played and for how long. The role and year are the two cells repeated verbatim in the reference, while the actual answer needs the ten-episode duration. Another asks about a 1995 event in Mexico City, but the source dates both races to 1955. A response that explains that inconsistency deserves credit. The sample is intentionally restricted to source-attested multi-fact answers. The paper’s wider benchmark population has a different distribution.
