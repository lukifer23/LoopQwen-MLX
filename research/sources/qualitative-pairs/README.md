# Reasoning about opposite outcomes from the same background

The previous model could gain exact-match points while losing source-correct answers. This training collection pairs 1,314 original QuaRTz questions with the authors’ published polarity-changing counterparts. Every pair shares the same background ID, normalized background and two answer options. The correct answer changes between its two situations. A constant answer per pair can solve at most half the individual questions and none of the complete pairs.

The 2,628 questions cover 281 backgrounds and 442 pairs of answer options. For 1,304 pairs the word-set Jaccard similarity between the two questions is at least 0.50. Each original source answer position is correct 1,314 times. We preserve the authors’ actual question, background, full options, answer text, answer position and flip IDs. The model can train on a full textual answer without predicting an arbitrary option letter. Authors and annotators supply the examples. The generation of a free-form reasoning explanation would require additional evidence.

Only the official training file was opened. Ten source records shared a whole field or a contiguous eight-word sequence with a question in the frozen 340-request panel. Eleven fell outside the source scope and another 47 eligible questions lacked a qualifying twin. The source has 2,696 train records. These cases are prospective supervision and did not enter the registered conditional model fit. Derivation and grouping are reproducible with `.venv/bin/python -m scripts.qualitative_training_pairs`.
